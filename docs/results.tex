\noindent\textbf{Result status.} The implemented SSA solver has executed paired,
short synthetic fixed-front and advancing-front simulations. Their initial and
post-spinup states agree, the front follows its prescribed offsets, thickness
stays positive, and the discrete mass budget closes to floating-point precision.
A calibrated historical EKaS simulation has not been executed. No agreement
with Dachauer et al. is claimed. The requested OOI-Image template was not found;
its exact AGENTS.md and plotting style remain pending. Figure formatting is provisional.

\section{Scientific comparison}
Dachauer et al. (2026), doi:\href{https://doi.org/10.1017/jog.2026.10172}{10.1017/jog.2026.10172},
Figure 13, examines how prescribed front advance changes flux and thickness at
a gate approximately 1 km upstream of the 1953 front. This implementation
compares a fixed-front control with a prescribed advancing front over 1953--2021,
after a common 25-year relaxation. The initialization is nominally 2021 geometry
truncated at the 1953 terminus; it is not a measured 1953 ice surface.
The publication's inversion, two-year relaxation and re-inversion are not reproduced.
The approximate offsets used here are 0, 960, 1400 and 1820 m in 1953, 1985,
2007 and 2021. These integrate rounded Figure 13 rates rather than digitized
front coordinates. Monotone PCHIP provides smooth position; piecewise-linear
interpolation is also available to match the paper's interpolation convention.

\section{Physics and domain motion}
Icepack's \texttt{IceStream} supplies SSA stress balance, Glen exponent $n=3$,
ocean traction and the hydrostatic surface convention. A prepared MAR 2021
annual SMB field is held fixed in both experiments. BedMachine Greenland v5
is the stock bed/surface source; a supplied corrected bed can replace it.
The authors' exact kriged bed and inverted fields were not obtained.
The model uses a straight 30 km by 3 km trunk corridor with observed inflow;
this differs from the paper's full ice-divide-bounded basin and curved fronts.

With speed $v$, basal coefficient $k=C_{\rm Ua}^{-1/3}$ and effective pressure
$N$, the implemented sliding stress approaches
\[
 t_b=\frac{\mu N k v^{1/3}}{\mu N+k v^{1/3}},\qquad
 N=\max\{\rho_i gH-\rho_w g\max(-z_b,0),0\}.
\]
Here $z_b=s-H$ is the ice base, including floating geometry. Floating ice has
zero basal traction. Icepack expects a dissipation potential rather than a
traction expression, so a 16-point quadrature integrates this stress over speed.
A speed regularization of $0.001$ m/year avoids the zero-speed singularity.
The default $\mu=0.5$ and uniform $A=10$ in MPa--m--year units are configurable
assumptions, not recovered Ua parameters. A provided friction raster or a
Weertman driving-stress prior is used without a parameter inversion.

Each timestep creates a new triangular corridor mesh of prescribed length.
Identical topology and node ordering allow thickness transfer with the old/new
length ratio, preserving total ice volume. Implicit continuity transport uses
$\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{w}$, physical ice velocity minus mesh velocity.
The split update is first-order and must be checked for temporal and spatial
convergence. Boundary mass flux is evaluated relative to the moving boundary;
any inflow uses the supplied initial thickness. No calving prediction or ad hoc
new ice slab is imposed. CG1 diagnostics use the actual element connectivity.
Nonpositive or nonfinite thickness aborts a run rather than being clipped.

\section{Executed checks}
The available local Docker image \texttt{vdv-ice-shelf} contains working
Firedrake and UW Icepack. A synthetic bed, surface and zero SMB were used with
a 12 by 4 mesh, $\Delta t=0.1$ year, a 0.1-year spinup and 0.2-year post-spinup
transient. This is a numerical test rather than a regional climate experiment.
The scientific default remains a 25-year relaxation and a 68-year transient.

At the final synthetic timestep, advancing-minus-control gate thickness is
0.0604 m and gate speed is -13.700 m/year. The signs indicate short-term thickening
and slowdown in this particular test. They do not demonstrate a stable
68-year historical response or reproduce the magnitude of Figure 13.
The paired validator confirmed equal input hashes, equal initial nodal states,
equal post-spinup sampled fields, stationary control front, prescribed advancing
front, finite positive thickness and small mass residuals. Two independent
unit tests checked chronology and mixed-law limits. Additional Firedrake checks
verify linear-element sampling, exact volume-preserving transfer and the
potential derivative against the intended mixed-law stress.

\section{Reproduction and remaining limitations}
\texttt{data/README.md} records required sources, units, masks and a GeoTIFF
preparation manifest. Raw and processed regional datasets are ignored by git.
The notebook retains executed synthetic outputs and explicitly reports the
absence of historical results. \texttt{make\_figure13.py} plots actual saved gate
curves and maps; it refuses incomplete historical runs and requires explicit
synthetic labeling. The figure comparison subtracts each case's post-spinup
baseline before subtracting control from advancing-front change.

For a defensible EKaS comparison, prepare verified regional geometry, MAR 2021
SMB and MEaSUREs 2021 velocity; calibrate the friction and rate factor; replace
approximate front offsets with mapped positions; and assess the corrected bed.
Then run both full experiments, halve timestep and mesh spacing, and compare
flux, thickness, velocity and mass budgets. The complete-run validator tests
thickening and slowdown without forcing their signs. Lack of agreement should
be documented rather than tuned away without an observational basis.

The full basin, inverted A/C fields, exact bed correction, and curved front
geometry are major differences from Ua. Corridor stretching and CG1 implicit
transport may add numerical diffusion. The qualitative historical response,
long-run stability and quantitative agreement remain unvalidated. The next
scientific step is explicit sediment thickness and bank geometry, after
establishing the geometric control experiment.
